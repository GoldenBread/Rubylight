# Windows service
- **The service starts even when its log cannot be rotated.** A service log that had reached its size limit and could not be moved aside (another program holding it open) used to stop the service from starting; it now keeps writing to the same file, and the older service logs stay as they were instead of being dropped one by one. A log that cannot be rotated at all is cut back at twice its size limit instead of growing, which drops the lines in that file; the log then says so in a line of its own, with how much was removed.
- **The service's log comes back after a rotation that could not reopen it.** When the log had been moved aside but the new file could not be opened, later writes pushed the file being written through the older logs until it was deleted; the next line now opens the log again, and no line is lost.
- **The service starts and runs without a log file when its log cannot be opened at all.** A read-only `service.log`, a folder in its place, a program holding it without letting others write, or a file in place of the `logs` folder used to stop the service from starting, and with it the host. The service now starts without its log and tries it again every minute.
- **A service log deleted or moved away while the service runs comes back, also when its folder was deleted with it.** The folder and the log are made again with the next line the service writes once ten seconds have passed; lines written in between went into the deleted file and are lost. While the log cannot be opened again, the service goes on in `service.log.0`, which rolls over with the older logs and stays within their size limit.
