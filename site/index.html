From desk to couch, without the fuss

Ready to play in minutes

Open https://localhost:47990 on your PC and Home guides pairing and your first stream: scan a QR code in Nightjar or enter a PIN in any Moonlight client. Press Ctrl+K anywhere to jump to any game, device or setting.

When Windows, capture or the network misbehaves, Help reads your host log automatically and pinpoints what happened—from encoder hangs to firewall or display issues—with concrete steps to fix it.

Every launcher, tuned per game & device

Steam, Epic Games, Battle.net, EA app, Ubisoft Connect, GOG, Xbox, Playnite and custom folders appear together with cover art and categories—nothing to type manually.

Attach Radeon or NVIDIA driver profiles (Competitive, Smoothness, Quality or custom rules), RTSS frame caps, per-device game settings files and optional frame generation cleanly per game and device, with automatic launch-focus recovery and guaranteed restore when you finish.

Live visibility into every frame

While streaming, Home reveals live frame rate, bitrate changes, capture backend and stage-by-stage timing. If motion ever hitches, Rubylight isolates whether time was lost in capture, GPU conversion, encoding, packetization or network send—and keeps history under Sessions.

Need deeper checks? Enable the per-frame timing CSV and inspect your own PC’s exact timestamps side by side with client telemetry.

Engineered for latency & smoothness

Built to keep every frame fresh and even

Rather than chasing static lab charts that vary across drivers and networks, Rubylight attacks delay and stutter at every stage of the live frame pipeline—and exposes real-time per-stage attribution so you can verify your own setup directly.

01 · CAPTURE

Direct Driver Push

Rubylight’s virtual display driver passes Windows-composed frames directly to the GPU encoder path; physical monitors use WGC with automatic DDX recovery.

02 · COMPUTE

Async D3D12 Work

Scaling, letterboxing, HDR scene brightness analysis and YUV conversion execute on D3D12 compute queues without stalling your game’s rendering queue.

03 · TIMING

VRR & Phase Lock

Waits briefly for the next game present under VRR instead of sending stale images, while matching arrivals right ahead of Nightjar’s display latch.

04 · ENCODE & WIRE

Slicing & Wavelets

Native AMF & NVENC plus 4K slice delivery, early FEC block dispatch and beam-raced PyroWave get pixels onto the wire before full-frame encode ends.

05 · ADAPT & VERIFY

Clean Recovery

Bounded Wi-Fi burst pacing, congestion feedback, oversize scene-cut protection, no-keyframe LTR recovery and per-frame timing logs eliminate mystery stutters.

What separates Rubylight

End-to-end architecture, complete control

Native AMF & NVENC

Drives each vendor’s encoder directly without generic wrappers, with per-codec VMAF-tuned AMF defaults, startup probes, and visible failures instead of silent fallbacks.

First-party virtual display

Per-device displays matched to each screen’s exact resolution, fractional refresh rate and HDR capabilities—restoring your physical monitors reliably afterwards.

First-party UMDF2 gamepads

Virtual DualSense (adaptive triggers & motion), Switch Pro, Xbox Series/One and wired 2026 Steam Controller built from source—no external controller driver hunt.

True HDR & calibration

10-bit HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision and HLG with GPU per-scene metadata, Windows SDR white brightness matching and clean 10-bit SDR fallback when displays stay SDR.

Full-chroma 4:4:4 & PyroWave

Razor-sharp HUDs and text via PyroWave HDR 4:4:4, packed 4:4:4 HEVC/AV1 for Nightjar, or native hardware 4:4:4 H.264/HEVC on capable NVIDIA GPUs.

Frame Warp & Real VRR

Shift camera motion onto Nightjar’s next refresh while anchoring the HUD, or present every decoded picture on the game’s exact PC rhythm on VRR displays.

Radeon & NVIDIA profiles

Per-game Competitive, Smoothness and Quality presets plus mid-stream Anti-Lag, Radeon Chill, NVIDIA Reflex/Low Latency and Smooth Motion—reverted cleanly on exit.

RTX HDR, RTX Video & FG

Optional NVIDIA RTX HDR tone-lifting, RTX Video sharpening and optional per-game frame generation mods across both vendors, with transparent pre-checks and uninstall.

Launch focus & RTSS caps

Brings fullscreen games back if minimised or trapped behind Steam during mode switches, keeps per-device game settings files separate and integrates cleanly with RTSS.

Real-network protection

Packet-arrival congestion control, bounded Wi-Fi burst pacing, oversize scene-cut keyframe re-encoding, no-keyframe LTR recovery and seamless client IP roaming.

WebRTC `/play` & guests

PIN-paired streaming straight into desktop browsers and Samsung TVs with HDR and surround, plus one-click guest links for up to four remote spectators or players.

4-player co-op & background

Run up to four independent split-screen co-op instances on one PC with persistent player slots, or stream cleanly in the background while someone else uses your desktop.

Quick Suspend & Stream DVR

Pause a running game between sessions so it resumes where you left off, and save instant replay clips or full recordings straight from the console or Nightjar’s Control Center.

Couch menu & party audio

Open the host-drawn couch menu with L3+R3 on any Moonlight client for Radeon toggles and session controls, and route per-player microphones and party voice cleanly.

USB/IP, clipboard & files

Bidirectional text & image clipboard sync, shared folders, paced file transfers, DualSense USB haptics/audio, Steam Deck controls and Switch 2 Pro over USB/IP.

Play anywhere & wake relay

Tailscale integration, UPnP/PCP/NAT-PMP router mapping, IPv6 reachability, Wake-on-LAN across NICs, always-on wake relays and serverless P2P traversal without port forwarding.

Sound up to 7.1.4 & passthrough

Opus surround up to 7.1.4, PCM / Dolby Digital / Dolby Digital Plus receiver passthrough, game-only audio isolation, night mode and couch microphone.

Obsidian Studio console

Pure Rust host, service and installer paired with a dark/light console featuring display topology, per-device override cascades, QR pairing, Ctrl+K search and redacted support bundles.

Why Rubylight exists

Built around the whole frame path

Radeon owners spent years being told their GPUs were inherently worse at game streaming. Almost always, the encoder wasn’t the culprit—everything around it was. Rubylight started as a clean Rust rewrite of the entire host pipeline around that path: capture, compute conversion, timing, recovery and network pacing.

Today that same architecture drives both AMD AMF and NVIDIA NVENC alongside first-party virtual display and controller drivers—giving either GPU predictable latency, smooth frame cadence and clean system restoration.
